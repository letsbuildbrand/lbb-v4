01 | Brand Foundation

Who Purosatva is

This strategy is built on a powerful emotional truth: urban families have lost their connection with nature, with farms, and with animals. There was once a time when people either owned cattle or at least knew where their milk came from. Today, milk has become a packaged commodity with fancy labels, but no real connection.

Brand Name
Purosatva
शुद्ध + सात्त्व | Purity in nature. The name itself is the promise.
Core Industry
Premium Dairy
Not milk delivery. Cow ownership. Farm connection. Community.
Target Audience
Urban families in Kolhapur district who value health and quality. Parents aged 28–45 who grew up with a connection to villages and farming. Families who feel the quiet loss of authenticity in modern food but do not know how to reclaim it. Children who know every brand on their phone but have never touched a cow.
Brand Voice
Calm. Confident. Deeply rooted. Never loud, never salesy, never desperate. The brand speaks like someone who knows their value and is simply inviting the right people to be part of it. Emotion leads. Logic supports. Visuals complete.

But this is never how it is communicated. The communication feels like belonging, like a family tie, like owning a piece of something real and alive.

03 | The EMOC Framework

Every post must carry all four elements

This is not optional. The EMOC framework creates psychological completeness | the audience's brain receives emotion, owns a piece of content, understands the logic, and receives a direction. That is what builds memory and trust.

E
Emotion
A feeling that the audience did not know they were carrying. The quiet ache of a lost connection. The warmth of a memory from childhood. The longing for something pure. Emotion opens the door before logic walks in.
Why it matters
People do not buy what makes sense. They buy what makes them feel something. Emotion is the hook. Without it, no post survives the scroll.
M
Memory
A piece of the brand or the founder's story that grounds everything in something real. The cow named something. The morning when the first calf was born. Memory makes the brand human and the philosophy lived.
Why it matters
People invest emotionally in stories before they invest financially. Memory creates parasocial trust | the feeling that you know the people behind the product.
O
Observation
A quiet question or reframing of something ordinary. "Do you really know where your milk comes from?" Observation disrupts the mental model | makes them pause and reconsider what they accepted as normal.
Why it matters
The quietest disruption is the most powerful. When you question something without attacking it, you create space for curiosity. Curiosity is the beginning of trust.
C
Connection
The invitation. Not to buy | but to belong. To be part of the Satva Circle. To visit the farm. To meet the cow. Connection is the message that moves people from audience to community.
Why it matters
The final frame is never a CTA. It is a door left open. "If this sounds like your family | we would love to meet you."
EMOC in action Emotion: A parent pours milk for their child who barely reacts. Memory: The founder remembers the cow in his village. Observation: "When did the milk we drink stop meaning something?" Connection: "Your family could be part of the Satva Circle. Come meet your cow."
04 | Content Architecture

Three layers of content

Every piece of content falls into one of three layers. The ratio is intentional: the cinematic creates desire, the raw creates trust, the graphic creates clarity. Together they build a complete world.

🎬
Cinematic / Film
50% of content
Beautiful, slow, deliberate. Drone shots of the farm at dawn. Early morning routines. Hands washing a cow's face. Cinematic content is the visual heartbeat | it makes the brand feel aspirational and real. Music-driven. Almost like short films.
📱
Raw / UGC Style
30% of content
Unpolished, real, warm. Shot on phone. Natural light. Children laughing. Hands on fur. Milk being poured. Raw content is the emotional texture | it makes the cinematic feel grounded and authentic.
🖼️
Graphic / Static
20% of content
Clean, minimal, text-first. Thought-provoking lines. Myth-busting content. Simple visual language. Graphic content is the intellectual clarity | it reinforces concepts without overwhelming the audience.
05 | Campaign Phases

Four phases of perception

The audience moves through four emotional stages. Each phase has a specific psychological goal and a specific tone. The content plan is designed to guide them through all four | slowly, gently, inevitably.

Phase 1 Days 1–10

The Uncomfortable Question

"Do you really know where your milk comes from?"

Make them pause. Create doubt. Disrupt their existing mental model of "premium milk." The content never mentions Purosatva as a product. It asks questions they have never been asked before.
Tone: Questioning, reflective, unsettling
Phase 2 Days 11–20

The Honest Answer

"There is another way."

Introduce the philosophy. Let emotion breathe. Build trust through transparency. The founder's story is told in full. The farm is revealed. The concept of cow ownership is planted | not as a financial model, but as a lifestyle upgrade.
Tone: Warm, revealing, philosophical
Phase 3 Days 21–26

The Invitation

"This is not for everyone. But it could be for you."

Position the Satva Circle as aspirational. Create desire through community, not product. The exclusivity is framed as care | "we are choosing 20 families carefully because relationships cannot scale." The door opens.
Tone: Aspirational, exclusive, generous
Phase 4 Days 27–30

The Quiet Call

"The door is open. Step in."

Soft conversion. Not a sales push. An open door. The call to action is warm, simple, human. Nothing feels like a billboard. Everything feels like a handwritten note.
Tone: Intimate, closing, reflective
Phase psychology Phase 1 plants a question. Phase 2 answers it with a story. Phase 3 makes the story feel like a world worth entering. Phase 4 lets them choose to step in. You never sell. You invite. The audience never buys. They belong.
